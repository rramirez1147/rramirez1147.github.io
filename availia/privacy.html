EVENTOS

Privacy Policy

Effective November 4, 2026

Eventos helps groups find a time to meet. It’s made by Ricardo Ramirez. This policy explains what Eventos stores, who can see it, and how long it’s kept. The short version: Eventos only stores what it needs to schedule, nobody sees your free times, Eventos never reads your calendar, and scheduling details are deleted soon after an event ends.

Your account

  • You sign in with Apple. Eventos receives your name (the first time only) and an email address, which can be a private relay address if you choose Hide My Email.
  • Eventos stores your name and time zone so friends see who you are and times appear correctly.
  • Friends who answer from an invitation link don’t create an account. They confirm the name the host entered (or change it), and their browser gets a temporary guest sign-in that’s removed once their events are cleaned up.

Events and groups

  • When you create an event, Eventos stores its name, notes, location, dates, preferred days and times, and the names of the people you invite.
  • Groups store their name and members.
  • Events can also have a place picked from Apple Maps, a Bring list (and who’s bringing what), and updates from the host.
  • Earlier versions let hosts add a cover photo. Any photos already added stay private to the event’s guests and are deleted when the host deletes their account.
  • People in an event can see its details, its place, the Bring list, updates, and who’s invited. People in a group can see its members.

Your availability

  • The times you add as free are stored for that event. Only you can see them. Eventos’s scheduling engine reads everyone’s times privately and shares only the times that work best and how many people can make each one.
  • Eventos doesn’t read your calendar. When a time is set, Add to Calendar opens Apple’s own event screen, already filled in, and you choose whether to save it. Eventos can’t see your calendars or what’s in them.

Contacts

  • If you allow access to Contacts, Eventos searches them on your iPhone as you type a name, so you can invite people quickly. Your contacts are never uploaded.
  • When you invite someone, only their name is sent, so the host and guests can see who’s invited. Their phone number or email stays on your iPhone and is only used to fill in the invitation text or email you choose to send.
  • You can invite people by typing their name without allowing access, and you can turn access off anytime in Settings › Privacy & Security › Contacts.

Maps

When you search for a place, your search goes to Apple Maps. Eventos doesn’t use your own location.

Notifications

If you allow notifications, Eventos stores your device’s push token to send updates about your events, delivered through Apple. You can turn reminders off in Profile › Notifications, or all notifications in Settings.

How long things are kept

  • 14 days after an event ends, everyone’s availability, the invitation links, the Bring list, the host’s updates, and the times that weren’t chosen are deleted automatically. The event’s name, who was invited, and the final time are kept so your group can see past events.
  • Guest sign-ins are removed once none of their events need them.
  • Notifications are deleted after 90 days.
  • Your account is kept until you delete it.

Deleting your data

Delete your account anytime in Profile › Delete Account. This removes your account, your availability, any cover photos you added, the groups you own, and the events you organize. On other people’s events your name stays on the guest list. Guests who answered from a link can email to have their responses removed.

Who processes data

  • Supabase hosts Eventos’s database in the United States.
  • Apple handles Sign in with Apple, delivers notifications, and provides Maps search.
  • GitHub Pages hosts this website and the invitation pages.

What Eventos doesn’t do

  • No advertising, analytics, or tracking.
  • No selling or sharing of your information.
  • No uploading of your contacts or your calendar.

Children

Eventos isn’t directed at children under 13 and doesn’t knowingly collect information from them.

Changes
